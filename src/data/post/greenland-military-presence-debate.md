## Question

 The United States federal government should substantially increase its military presence in Greenland

🤖:[claude-opus-5*]
### The clash in one line

Both sides agree the Arctic matters and that the U.S. is staying in Greenland. The fight is over **whether more American boots and bases buy security, or buy resentment that costs us more security than it buys.**

### Where you draw the line

**Capability vs. consent.** A bigger U.S. footprint adds real hardware; it also converts a shared allied project into an American occupation-by-degrees in the eyes of 56,000 Greenlanders and the whole of Europe. The line is: *how much presence can we add before the political cost outruns the military gain?*

Gov says the line is drawn by **geography and threat** — you put force where the missiles and sea lanes are.
Opp says the line is drawn by **invitation** — presence that allies ask for is durable, presence they resent is a liability we have to re-buy every election.

**Critical framing note:** this resolution just got overtaken by events. On September 18, 2026 Trump announced an agreement with Greenland and Denmark that he described as giving the U.S. "permanent control over security," saying the U.S. would "immediately begin the process of developing a large Military presence". Whoever adapts faster wins. Gov should argue the deal proves the plan is feasible and consented-to. Opp should argue the deal *is* the status quo, so the resolution means going *beyond* it — and that's where harms live.

### Government — 5 points

**1. Geography you can't substitute.** The shortest flight path from Russia to North America runs over Greenland. Pituffik Space Base, built after the 1951 Defense of Greenland Treaty, supports missile warning, missile defense and space surveillance for the U.S. and NATO. No other real estate does that job.
→ *Opp responds:* we already have that base and the treaty rights. You're describing the status quo, not the resolution.

**2. The ice is melting, and the map is changing.** New shipping routes and seabed resources mean Arctic traffic that nobody currently polices. Presence now is cheaper than a crisis later.
→ *Opp responds:* icebreakers, radar and satellites police sea lanes — infantry battalions don't. You're buying the wrong tool.

**3. The burden gap is real.** Denmark's Arctic patrol capacity is genuinely thin for an island with ~27,000 miles of coastline. If the ally can't cover it, the U.S. either covers it or leaves a vacuum.
→ *Opp responds:* they're fixing it — see the spending facts below. Don't crowd out an ally who's finally paying.

**4. Deterrence is about visible commitment.** Russia and China read force posture, not communiqués. A thin tripwire invites probing; a substantial presence forecloses it.
→ *Opp responds:* there is no Russian plan to invade Greenland. You're deterring a threat you can't name, while creating a security dilemma that militarizes the Arctic.

**5. Minerals and supply chains.** Greenland's rare earths matter to defense manufacturing; a secure presence protects the infrastructure and keeps Chinese investment out.
→ *Opp responds:* that's an economic interest wearing a uniform. Buy the minerals; don't garrison the seller.

### Opposition — 5 points

**1. You already have the right, so "substantially increase" adds cost, not capability.** A 1951 U.S.–Denmark defense agreement already gives Washington broad military rights in Greenland. Nothing in the status quo blocks the U.S. — so the resolution is buying scale, not access.
→ *Gov responds:* rights unused are rights wasted; the resolution is about actually exercising them.

**2. The method poisoned the well.** One Atlantic Council analyst said Trump's demands "rattled the NATO alliance," that Americans "underestimate how damaging it was for the image of the United States in Europe," and that allies saw it as an attack on sovereignty that will take work to repair. Alliances are the actual American advantage; spending them for one island is a bad trade.
→ *Gov responds:* the deal got signed anyway, and it recognizes Danish sovereignty and Greenlandic self-determination. Allies grumble, then cooperate.

**3. Consent of 56,000 people.** Greenland has roughly 56,000 residents, and Greenland has maintained it is not for sale. A base economy that the population resents produces protests, restrictions, and eventual eviction — see Okinawa.
→ *Gov responds:* Greenland's own PM called the arrangement beneficial. Consent exists; you're speaking for people who spoke for themselves.

**4. Crowding out an ally who is finally paying.** Denmark committed roughly \$2 billion in early 2025, then agreed with Greenland and the Faroe Islands on a Second Agreement on the Arctic and North Atlantic of 2025 worth DKK 27.4 billion (about USD 4.26 billion). Substituting American forces removes the incentive that produced that money.
→ *Gov responds:* that spending happened *because* of U.S. pressure. Our presence is the forcing function.

**5. Escalation spiral.** Every U.S. base north of the Circle justifies a Russian one. You end up less safe in an Arctic full of forward-deployed forces and no crisis-management channels.
→ *Gov responds:* Russia reopened Arctic bases first. We're responding, not initiating.

### The 4 facts to memorize

| Fact | Use |
|---|---|
| 1951 Defense of Greenland Treaty → Pituffik Space Base; missile warning, missile defense, space surveillance | Opp's "you already have access"; Gov's "geography is irreplaceable" |
| Sept 18, 2026 deal: "permanent control over security," a "large Military presence" — but stops short of making Greenland part of the U.S. | Status-quo definition; both sides |
| Denmark's Second Arctic Agreement 2025: DKK 27.4 billion ≈ \$4.26 billion | Opp burden-sharing; Gov "we caused it" |
| Greenland's population: ~56,000 | Opp consent/scale; also shows how few people bear the footprint |

### Weighing — how to actually win this round

Gov wins on **magnitude and irreversibility**: a missile-warning gap can't be fixed after the fact.
Opp wins on **probability and link turn**: no named aggressor, and the plan trades a certain alliance cost for a speculative military gain.

One tip: if you're Opp, don't defend "do nothing." Counter-advocate *allied* presence — in January 2026 France, Germany, Sweden, Norway and Britain sent personnel to Greenland for a short deterrence exercise. That lets you capture Gov's deterrence offense without the sovereignty cost, and it's the single hardest thing for Gov to answer in 20 minutes of prep.
