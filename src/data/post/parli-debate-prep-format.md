Sounds good. Here's the template I'll use so we can calibrate now rather than mid-topic.

### Output template per topic

1. **The clash in one line** — the single question the round actually turns on, framed as a line-drawing problem ("where do we draw the line on A vs. B").
2. **The line-drawing axis** — what each side has to prove to win that line, and the usual burden split.
3. **Gov (Prop) — 5 points** — each as claim → warrant → why it matters, one sentence each.
4. **Opp — 5 points** — same structure, and where possible directly mirrored against a Gov point so you can see the head-on collision.
5. **Most likely responses** — for each side, the 2–3 turns/answers the other team will almost certainly run, so nothing surprises you.
6. **≤5 hard facts** — mainstream-press-verifiable, with links. Used as anchors, not as a card dump.

I'll keep the kitchen sink out. If a topic genuinely has two independent contentions (e.g., a rights clash *and* an efficacy clash), I'll say so and recommend which one to pick a fight on, rather than listing both at equal weight.

### Two calibration questions

- **Motion type.** Parli motions come in fact ("X caused Y"), value ("X is more important than Y"), and policy ("The USFG should…") flavors, and the clash structure differs a lot. Do you want me to infer the type from how you phrase the topic, or will you tell me? Default: I infer, and say which I assumed.
- **Framework layer.** Do you want a suggested weighing mechanism (e.g., net benefits, or a value/criterion pair like "justice → protection of the vulnerable")? For high school parli this often wins rounds, but it's also the part that can become kitchen-sink. Default: one sentence, no more.

One honest caveat: since parli is prep-limited and no-evidence-reading, my facts are for *your* understanding and for credible paraphrase in-round — not for citation as cards. I'll flag anything where the mainstream framing is genuinely contested rather than settled.

Give me the first topic whenever you're ready.
